Case study · 01

Membria CE learns from reverts

Solo developers · Claude Code, Codex, Cursor · SaaS
1.
The situation
A developer asks the agent to add caching to an API client. The agent reaches for an in-memory LRU — the same choice that broke under multi-worker deploys and was reverted two weeks ago.
2.
How it works
Before choosing, the agent asks Membria for decision context. It gets the reverted decision with its lesson (per-process cache, inconsistent across workers) and a signal from the commons: most projects that adopt this pattern later remove it.
3.
The result
The agent picks a shared cache and records the decision with its alternatives. When the change survives, the outcome attaches itself from git and CI — nobody writes a post-mortem.

Case study · 02

Membria CLI agents as one team

Engineering teams · inside the customer perimeter · On-Premise
1.
The situation
A team of six has to add rate limiting to the public API. Instead of six separate agent sessions, the task enters one pipeline: Researcher, Planner, Developer, Reviewer and Gate.
2.
How it works
The Researcher surfaces a per-process counter the team shipped and reverted last year. The Planner builds the plan against CodeGraph and adds a load test. An engineer's own Claude Code takes the Developer seat over MCP.
3.
The result
The Gate blocks the first attempt for missing load-test evidence and passes the second. Every step has an actor, a model, a policy decision and an audit record, and the team graph never leaves the perimeter.

Case study · 03

AI-EPC from tender to issue

EPC contractors & design institutes · SaaS
1.
The situation
An EPC contractor uploads a tender folder: specifications, drawings, vendor catalogues, contract terms. This is where requirements get missed, and a late clause turns into rework — 28% of budget overruns.
2.
How it works
AI-EPC reads every file, scanned pages included, into a requirements register where each line cites its source clause. It builds the design level by level: models propose, rules, codes and catalogues accept or reject, and unknown values become questions to the customer.
3.
The result
Each discipline lead approves documents at a gate, and a change in one place reaches every dependent sheet. The contractor issues a consistent package with an evidence trail behind each document. Engineers sign; the system never stamps.

Case study · 04

Crypto broker one data bus

Licensed virtual-asset broker · Central Asia · client anonymised · On-Premise
1.
The situation
A licensed broker runs execution and compliance as two parallel tracks built by two vendors. An operation must not execute until compliance has decided on it, and personal data must stay out of shared systems.
2.
How it works
Membria owns the data bus and decision memory between the tracks: a Kafka backbone with 38 schema-validated event types and a graph of 42 object types. A client's status and every operation decision travel to the other track over the bus.
3.
The result
A compliance officer asks in plain language why an operation was blocked and gets the full chain — screening result, limit breaches, approval steps — with the source events. Both vendors integrate against one documented contract.

Case study · 05

Bank reporting & mortgage

Developer-owned bank · regulatory reporting, mortgage and escrow · On-Premise
1.
The situation
Methodologists spend 2–5 business days on each central-bank report specification. Mortgage review is a 40+ field manual checklist, and escrow specs are copied from the previous project.
2.
How it works
A reporting assistant writes the specification from a free-form task, using graphs of the bank's object model and the regulator's forms. Skills keep the mappings that worked; rejected mappings are never proposed again. A mortgage assistant checks each application against project and escrow data.
3.
The result
A specification is ready in 1–4 hours instead of 2–5 days, and when the regulator changes a form, every affected specification is listed at once. The bank picks its own model — Claude, GigaChat or on-prem Qwen — and data stays inside its security boundary.

Case study · 06

C2 Center decisions in minutes

Government emergency response · On-Premise
1.
The alert
Drone LiDAR shows slope movement near a bridge well above the seasonal limit, while river levels rise. The duty officer needs one decision-ready picture, not five separate systems.
2.
How it works
The Duty agent matches the finding with the object's history in the knowledge graph and raises a risk alert with a confidence score and cited sources. The Planner offers three response options, Routes builds detours, and quality control blocks any option that repeats a recorded unsafe decision.
3.
The result
The chief of staff approves, and only then do alerts go out in two languages by SMS, Cell Broadcast and sirens. On closed roads, edge-AI cameras send plate events instead of video, with no vehicle-owner registry; the full trail stays on-premise.
